What is TOE?

Technology – Organization – Environment

TOE explains how a company adopts a new technology by looking at three contexts at once.

A classic model of innovation adoption (Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990): whether an innovation succeeds depends not only on the technology itself, but also on the organization that uses it and the environment it competes in.

The TOE Model

Three contexts shape one decision

Three questions for every AI decision

T · Technology

What can AI do for us?

Capabilities, data, integration and security

O · Organization

Are we ready to capture the value?

Strategy, governance, people, skills and change

E · Environment

What do market and regulator demand?

Competitors, customers, partners and regulation
02

The Three Dimensions

Seeing AI as a system, not as a tool

Dimension 1: Technology

T

What is now possible across our value chain?

  • Foundation models & agents: from copilots to autonomous workflows
  • Document & process automation: reading documents, extracting data, drafting answers
  • Conversational AI: sales, support and advice agents in the customer’s language
  • Integration & APIs: connecting AI to ERP, CRM and partner platforms
  • Data & analytics: forecasting, personalisation and smarter pricing
  • Security: model, data and agent attack surfaces

Dimension 2: Organization

O

How must the company change to capture value at scale?

  • AI strategy & governance: direction and guardrails
  • AI Center of Excellence: the delivery engine for AI use cases
  • People & skills: reskilling employees, managers and partners
  • Change management: adoption over resistance
  • New business models: AI-enabled products, services and revenue streams
  • KPIs & investment priorities: measuring AI operating leverage

Dimension 3: Environment

E

Which competitive and regulatory context shapes our strategy?

  • Competitor analysis: incumbents, digital challengers, big tech
  • Industry disruption: new entrants and AI-native business models
  • Regulation: industry rules and AI laws (e.g. EU AI Act)
  • Data protection & ethical AI: privacy and explainability as licence to operate
  • Ecosystem & partners: build / buy / partner, data sharing
  • Future outlook: agentic AI adoption rising fast towards 2028

Why Management Needs TOE

From board conviction to operating leverage

Why AI initiatives fail

What happens when one dimension is missing

Technology only

A great AI model without an operating model: pilots that never scale

Organization missing

No trained people, no sponsor, no budget: tools stay unused or turn into Shadow AI

Environment missing

Blind to regulators and competitors: solutions that are non-compliant or too slow

Leaders over-index on technology and under-invest in organization and environment.

What TOE is for

Six jobs the framework does for the management team

  • Shared language: board, C-suite and teams discuss AI with one structure
  • Balanced investment: budget for people, process and compliance, not only tools
  • Prioritisation: score every initiative on T, O and E readiness
  • Risk & governance: spot regulatory, data and ethical risks early
  • Roadmap: sequence initiatives across horizons with owners and gates
  • Communication: explain the AI agenda to board, investors and regulator

TOE in Practice

One lens for every AI initiative

Example: AI customer service agent

Answering customers 24/7, seen through the TOE lens

Technology

  • Understands questions in natural language
  • Grounded in product knowledge (RAG)
  • Connected to CRM and order systems
  • Evaluated for accuracy and tone

Organization

  • Support team trained to work with AI
  • Clear handover to a human
  • Owned by the AI Center of Excellence
  • KPI: resolution time & satisfaction

Environment

  • Customers know they talk to AI
  • Consent & data protection rules
  • Industry rules on advice & complaints
  • Competitors set the service benchmark

The TOE checklist

Ask these questions before approving any AI initiative

Technology

  • Is the technology mature for this use case?
  • Do we have the data?
  • Does it integrate with our systems?
  • Is it secure?

Organization

  • Who owns it, who uses it?
  • Are the people trained?
  • Which processes change?
  • How do we measure value?

Environment

  • What do the regulator and the law require?
  • What are competitors doing?
  • Which partners do we need?
  • How will customers react?

A “no” in any column is not a stop sign, it is the next investment to plan.
